# Hi, I'm Denis (aka Gelerion)

I'm a Software Architect at ServiceNow, based in Israel. I've spent over a
decade designing and building distributed systems, data platforms, and
high-performance backend services. These days, much of my work focuses on AI
platforms, agent architecture, and security.

Before ServiceNow, I worked as a Principal Software Engineer at Salesforce.

My interests have moved over the years from JVM internals and distributed data
processing to getting AI agents to work reliably and securely in production. The
questions I keep asking are the same: How does this actually work? What happens
when it fails? How could we design it better?

## What I write about

This blog is where I work through engineering problems, write down what I've
learned, and sometimes push back on approaches that look good on paper but fall
apart in practice.

You'll mostly find articles about:

- **AI agents and architecture:** agent runtimes, memory systems, RAG,
  orchestration, evaluation, and the move from prototype to production.
- **AI and application security:** agent permissions, access control, threat
  modeling, security analysis, and runtime protection.
- **Distributed systems and data engineering:** microservices, event-driven
  architectures, streaming pipelines, lakehouses, and reliable data platforms at
  scale.
- **Performance and production engineering:** JVM internals, profiling,
  concurrency, Kubernetes, observability, and lessons from real production
  systems.

I try to go past high-level concepts. Where I can, I dig into the mechanics,
work through concrete examples, and explain the trade-offs behind the
architectural decisions.

## How I think about engineering

I'm a big believer in pragmatic architecture. There's rarely a single right way
to build something, and the most sophisticated solution isn't necessarily the
best one.

I care about how systems behave under real workloads, where their boundaries
are, and what happens when assumptions break. Good abstractions matter, but so
do performance, observability, and being able to debug a problem when things go
wrong.

The same goes for AI systems. A clever prompt or a powerful model is only part
of it. An agent is trustworthy enough for production when its context,
permissions, reliability, and runtime controls are handled well.

Beyond engineering, I'm a husband, father of two, and gamer.
